01

The colour verdict

Keep it. #327882 — deep slate teal

It is the right choice for this product, and it is right for a reason worth understanding: it is a low-chroma colour (0.071 in OKLCH, where a saturated brand blue runs 0.15–0.20). Low chroma reads as considered rather than loud. On a product whose entire argument is trust my arithmetic, that is exactly the register you want.

Why it works here

It's not the AI colour. Every AI product in 2026 is violet-to-indigo. Teal reads finance-adjacent and calm, which is the rarer and more defensible position for software that touches money.

It's already yours. Your logo mark is built from it. The brand is coherent before you've written a rule down.

It behaves in both themes. Hue 208° stays recognisably itself from 5% to 95% lightness — many brand colours turn muddy or neon at the ends of the ramp. This one doesn't.

The one thing to watch

At 53% lightness it sits almost exactly in the middle of the range, which makes it a great fill and a mediocre text colour on dark backgrounds.

White on #327882 = 5.07:1 ✓ — buttons are fine.
#327882 on white = 5.07:1 ✓ — light-mode text is fine.
#327882 on #0A0B0F = 3.88:1 ✗ — fails AA.

Your current build uses #327882 as a text and label colour throughout the dark drawer. Fix: use #5DA5B0 (teal-400) for anything that is text on a dark surface. Same hue, reads as the same brand colour, 7.0:1 ✓.

The rule, in one line

teal-500 fills. teal-600 writes on light. teal-400 writes on dark. Three tokens, one decision, never think about it again.

Where it sits against the category

Worth knowing what you're standing next to: Odoo is violet, Zoho is red-and-multicolour, SAP is a corporate blue, NetSuite is a warm grey-blue, QuickBooks is a bright green, Xero is a bright cyan-blue. Teal-at-low-chroma is genuinely open ground in this category — the nearest neighbour is Xero, and Xero sits far brighter and far more saturated. You are not going to be mistaken for anyone.

ERP-specific rule

In a financial product, semantic colour is data, not decoration. Never use teal to mean "good" and never use the semantic greens or reds for a brand accent — the moment a user learns that green means reconciled, a green button becomes a lie. Keep the two systems completely separate.

And never encode meaning in colour alone: a red figure also gets a minus sign or a parenthesis, a passing test also gets a ✓. Roughly 1 in 12 men can't reliably separate your red from your green.

Found in your current build

In index.html, the customiser drawer sets every section label and every uppercase heading to text-[#327882] on a zinc-950 background. That's 3.88:1 — below AA for text at any weight under 18.66px bold. All of those labels are 9–11px. Swap the class to text-[#5DA5B0] and it passes at 7.0:1 with no visible change in brand feel.

Never do these

  • Pure black #000000 as a background — it makes every shadow invisible and every white text vibrate. #0A0B0F instead.
  • Pure white #FFFFFF as body text on dark — 19.7:1 is harsher than anything print would ever use. #F4F6F8 instead.
  • More than three greys in one view. If you need a fourth, your hierarchy is wrong, not your palette.
  • Teal on teal for text. Tint backgrounds (teal-50) take ink text, not teal text.
04

Gradients

Your current hero gradient runs dark → teal → near-white top to bottom, and flips in dark mode. It's ambitious and it's causing two problems: text colour has to invert with it (which is why the headline is white in light mode and black in dark mode — a rule nobody can maintain), and the near-white bottom kills the depth the top half worked to build.

CURRENT

Dark → teal → white. Full-value swing forces inverted text rules and washes out at the fold.

RECOMMENDED

linear-gradient(170deg,#0A0B0F 0%,#0C393F 46%,#21656F 74%,#327882 100%) — stays dark throughout, so text is white, always. Four stops instead of three kills banding.

MESH (hero)

Three offset radial gradients over #0A0B0F. Better than a linear behind a WebGL canvas because it has no directional seam to fight with.

The four gradient rules

1. Never cross the midpoint. A gradient that goes from dark to light forces you to invert text mid-section. Keep every gradient entirely in the dark half or entirely in the light half.
2. Three stops minimum, four preferred. Two-stop gradients band visibly on cheap monitors — which is what most of your buyers have.
3. Move hue, not just lightness. #0A0B0F → #327882 shifts hue 250° → 208°, which is why it feels like light rather than a fade.
4. One gradient per page. It's the hero's signature. A second one makes the first one ordinary.

About the fluid simulation

It's a beautiful piece of work and it should stay — a real-time WebGL fluid field is a legitimate "this was made by someone who cares" signal, and it costs you nothing at the top of the page. Four conditions on keeping it:

  • Do not let it cover the gradient. Right now #fluid-canvas sits at z-index:1 and .hero-gradient-overlay at z-index:0 — the canvas paints over the gradient, so the gradient underneath is doing no work. Put the gradient above the canvas with a low-alpha overlay, or drop the canvas to z-index:0 and set the overlay to a semi-transparent scrim.
  • Kill it on mobile. Continuous WebGL is a battery and thermal problem on a phone. Serve a static mesh gradient below 768px — nobody will know.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Freeze the sim and render one static frame. This is an accessibility requirement, not a nicety — motion like this triggers vestibular symptoms in some people.
  • Fade it out below the fold. It should feel like the top of the page, not like a background the whole site is sitting on.
05

Typography

Your current pairing — Inter with Instrument Serif italic accents — is genuinely good, and it's the part of the current design I'd change least. Keep both. Add one thing.

Inter

Everything. UI, headings, body, buttons. Weights 400 / 500 / 600 only — three weights is a system, five is a mess. Never use 700 for a heading; at display sizes 600 with tight tracking reads stronger and more expensive.

Instrument Serif

One word per page. Italic only. It's the brand's signature and it works because it's rare. Two on a page and it's a style; three and it's noise.

JetBrains Mono

Add this. Eyebrows, labels, data, code, version numbers, test counts. For an ERP a mono face does real work: it makes numbers look measured, which is the entire brand argument.

The detail that matters most for this product

Set font-feature-settings:"tnum" globally, or at minimum on every table, price, dashboard figure and report. Tabular figures make all digits the same width so columns of numbers align down the page. In a financial product, proportional figures in a table are the visual equivalent of a typo — and almost nobody remembers to switch them on.

Also worth setting on Inter: "cv11" (single-storey a alternate off / disambiguated 1lI) and "ss01". Small refinements, but they're what separates a site that used Inter from a site that specified Inter.

Type rules

  • Measure: 60–75 characters. Set max-width:72ch on paragraphs and never let a full-width container decide your line length.
  • Tracking is a function of size. ≥40px → −3%; 24–40px → −2.5%; 18–24px → −1.5%; body → 0; ≤13px uppercase → +12 to +16%.
  • One weight jump per hierarchy step. If 600 next to 400 isn't enough separation, change the size or the colour — not the weight.
  • Headlines break where the meaning breaks. Use <br> deliberately in hero copy; never let the browser choose. And never leave a one-word last line.
  • Self-host the fonts. woff2, font-display:swap, preloaded. Google Fonts' CDN costs you a DNS lookup, a connection and a render-blocking request on every first visit.

The minimalism rule

Minimal design is not fewer elements — it's more space between the elements you kept. When a section looks cluttered the instinct is to delete something; the fix is almost always to double the padding first. Your current hero already does this well. The footer doesn't: four link columns at 32px gaps read as dense. Take them to 64.

Your current build uses a custom 2.3rem (36.8px) radius. That's a lot of curve — it reads friendly-consumer, which fights the financial-precision argument. Cap at 24px for containers. Fully-round buttons are the exception and they're the right call: pill buttons against square-ish cards is a deliberate, good contrast.

Elevation

Four levels, no more. In light mode use shadow; in dark mode use surface lightness, not shadow — a black shadow on a near-black background is invisible, and this is the most common dark-mode mistake.

Grid

12 columns, 24px gutters, 32px page margin desktop / 20px mobile. Breakpoints: 640 · 768 · 1024 · 1280. Design mobile-first at 375px — over half your visitors will be on a phone, and an ERP landing page is exactly the kind of thing people evaluate on a phone and buy on a laptop.

07

Components

Buttons — light

Buttons — dark

  • One primary per view. Two primaries is no primary. Your current hero has "See Demo" and "Book a Call" competing at equal weight — pick one.
  • Height 44px minimum (48px on mobile). That's the touch-target floor, and it's not negotiable.
  • Padding 22px horizontal — buttons that hug their label look cheap.
  • Hover: darken one ramp step + translateY(-1px). Never scale a button on hover; it makes the layout feel unstable.
  • Focus: 2px offset ring in --accent. Your current CSS has a .no-focus-ring class on the hero textarea. Remove it — that's the keyboard equivalent of hiding the cursor.

Card rules: 1px border or a shadow, never both. 20–24px internal padding. Same height in a row — set the shortest card's copy length as the ceiling for all of them. Numbered eyebrows in mono for any sequence.

Inputs

Height 48px, radius 10px, 1px --line border, 16px internal padding. Font-size 16px minimum — anything smaller makes iOS Safari zoom on focus, which feels broken. Focus: border → --accent plus a 3px ring at 15% alpha. Labels sit above the field, always, in 13px --text-2; placeholder text is never a substitute for a label.

The hero prompt field is the exception and should stay as it is: borderless, transparent, one hairline underneath, 18–20px text. It should feel like writing, not like filling in a form. That instinct in your current build is correct.

Tables — the ERP-specific component

You will have more tables than any other component, and they are where a financial product either looks trustworthy or doesn't.

  • Numbers right-aligned, mono, tabular figures. Non-negotiable.
  • Labels left-aligned, sans. Never centre anything in a data table except a status chip.
  • Row height 48px, header 40px, header text 11px mono uppercase in --text-3.
  • Horizontal rules only — vertical lines make a table look like a spreadsheet, and the whole point is that you replaced the spreadsheet.
  • Negative numbers in --danger and in parentheses. Colour alone is not a signal.
  • Totals row: 1px --line above, weight 600, never a filled background.

Navigation

Height 64px (72px is generous, 96px — your current value — is a lot of screen given away before anyone has read a word). Transparent over the hero, then on scroll: rgba(255,255,255,.85) + backdrop-filter:blur(12px) + a 1px bottom border. Transition over 200ms. Logo 28px. Links 14px weight 500 in --text-2, going to --text on hover.

Motion

The rule for this brand: motion should read as mechanical precision, not playfulness. Things assemble, settle and lock. Nothing bounces, nothing wobbles, nothing overshoots — an accounting product that springs is telling you something untrue about itself.

  • Scroll reveal: opacity 0→1 + translateY(20px→0) over 480ms, 60ms stagger between siblings. Once only — never re-animate on scroll-up. Your existing .reveal-item already does this correctly; keep it, reduce the travel from 24px to 20px.
  • The Blueprint animation is the exception. That one should be slow and legible — modules appearing one at a time over ~2.5s total, with the state labels changing as it goes. Let people watch it. It's the product demo; rushing it wastes the only moment where motion is doing sales work.
  • Never animate: a number counting up on a financial figure (it reads as a slot machine), anything that shifts layout after paint, or the fluid sim below the fold.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: reveals become instant opacity fades, the fluid canvas renders one static frame, ambient loops stop. One media query, and it's an accessibility requirement rather than an option.
09

Nine fixes in the current build

In order of how much they cost you. The first four are the ones I'd do before showing the site to anyone.

Critical · 5 minutes
The customiser drawer ships to production

The floating settings panel with vorticity, splat force, pressure iterations and dye resolution controls is a development tool sitting on your marketing site. A prospective buyer's first impression of a financial system should not be a physics control panel. Gate it behind ?debug=1. Highest risk-to-effort ratio on this list.

Critical · rewrite
The copy is still a template

<title>VenQore — Design with AI</title>, "the future of creativity", "VenQore uses advanced AI to transform your ideas into stunning designs", a pricing page selling "50 design generations/month". None of this is your product. Everything in the copy bible replaces it.

Critical · 2 minutes
Dark-mode accent text fails contrast

text-[#327882] on zinc-950 is 3.88:1 across every label in the drawer and every uppercase section heading. Replace with #5DA5B0.

Critical · 1 minute
.no-focus-ring on the hero input

The single most important interactive element on the site is unreachable-looking by keyboard. Remove the class, style the focus state properly instead.

High · half a day
The dual-theme inversion is unmaintainable

The headline is text-white dark:text-black and the sub-text is text-black dark:text-white — because the gradient itself inverts. Every new section will need this reasoning applied by hand, and one of them will get it wrong. Go dark-hero / light-body, single theme, and keep the toggle for inside the product.

High · 20 minutes
Tailwind is loading from the CDN

cdn.tailwindcss.com is explicitly a prototyping tool — it compiles your CSS in the browser on every visit. It's a render-blocking script plus a JIT compile before anything paints. Build a real stylesheet before launch.

Medium · 30 minutes
Nav has seven links and no hierarchy

Features · Templates · Pricing · Resources · Contact · Join — plus a separator. "Templates" and "Join" aren't your product's language. Five links, two buttons, one primary CTA that says Start building.

Medium
Fluid canvas covers the gradient it's meant to sit on

#fluid-canvas is z-index:1, .hero-gradient-overlay is z-index:0. Whatever the gradient is doing, the canvas is on top of it. Restack, and add mobile + reduced-motion fallbacks while you're in there.

Low
Small things

Nav is 96px tall — take it to 64. The 4xl:2.3rem radius is too round for a financial product — cap containers at 24px. Footer watermark at text-[16vw] with mb-[12vw] is doing a lot; either commit to it as a signature or cut it. And every footer link currently points at #.

10

The one-paragraph summary

Keep the teal, keep Inter with the serif italic accent, keep the fluid hero — those three decisions are good and they're already yours. Add a mono face for numbers and turn on tabular figures, because that's what makes an ERP look like it can count. Drop to a single theme so you stop maintaining two versions of every section. Replace every word of the template copy with the positioning: the ERP that builds itself around your business, with double-entry accounting the AI can't touch. Then take the settings drawer off the production page before anyone sees it.
