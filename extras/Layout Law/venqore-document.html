“Keep the summary on the right side, or make the customer and details section collapsible at the top so the items get the middle and left space.” Both, as a switch. Collapsed is one line — party, number, date, running total — and it is worth 5 to 10 more visible item rows on a laptop. The readout prints the number so the trade is not a matter of opinion.
“When the user scrolls, show the summary in the bottom right corner with Complete sale — specially for the Pro density.” There, and Pro turns out to be a consequence rather than a preference: see below.
“Work on the tablet and phone screens, those are not good.” A summary column only survives while the line table beside it is still a table. Below that it moves under the lines, and on a phone it becomes the dock. The line table becomes cards, and a card opens its own controls in place when you tap it.
“Let the user decide how they want to have it.” Five knobs, six starting points, and a draggable divider. Every one of them is clamped by a measured floor rather than by a breakpoint.
Heights felt over-managed. Nothing is deleted to save height. When the height runs out the details collapse — one gesture from being back — and everything else scrolls.

Why the docked summary lands on Pro

You noticed it on the Pro density, and that is not a coincidence — it is arithmetic. Holding a panel still while the page scrolls past only works if the whole panel fits on screen; a sticky thing taller than its viewport still scrolls, it just scrolls late, which is the worst of both. And the summary's height is its density: the density list is literally the list of summary rows. Three for Simple, seven for Standard, ten for Pro plus the tax breakdown. So Pro is the first density that stops fitting, and the law names it without being told to.

And the dock is a row, not a float. The Counter register had a browse button floating over the payment panel, which is the same defect this could have: a summary card in the corner covering the last line of the table forever. So the dock's height is reserved at the bottom of the scroller before anything is measured. It is anchored to the right so the left of the last row stays visible, and the space it sits in belongs to it.

Three densities, and why the law can veto one

Density is a preference — an accountant wants ten line columns, a cashier wants five — but it is a preference the width has to be able to honour. The ten-column table needs 933px, measured from real advance widths. Below that the law caps the density and says so. A density is the only thing the width may veto; it may never veto a capability.

The nav now knows what you composed

Solving this the first time produced a rule that documents hold the rail until 1708px, because 192px of parked chrome is a whole table column and you do not navigate from an invoice. That number was derived from the default zone weights — and the sweep caught what happens when you change them: at 1708 a Pro ledger with a 32% summary lost its tenth line column the moment the nav expanded. Buying a bigger screen made the invoice worse again, one composition further along.

So the No-Regression Rule moved from derivation time to run time. The nav holds the rail wherever expanding it would cost this composition a fit — watch the pill in the header at 1708 with the Pro ledger preset.
