### Company name
VenQore

### One-line description
A multi-tenant ERP and POS system built around one obsessively-tested accounting core, so small businesses stop stitching together five tools and re-entering the same data into each one.

---

### What are you building?

VenQore is an ERP and POS system for small businesses, built around one central, heavily-tested accounting engine — every sale, purchase, and ledger entry runs through it, verified to 20 decimal places across 1,000+ automated tests, so the numbers are never inconsistent between modules. On top of that core, we're adding AI features that remove manual data entry: turning handwritten orders, screenshots, and voice notes into real sales, and syncing inventory automatically across every channel a business sells on. The goal is one system instead of five disconnected subscriptions.

---

### What problem are you solving?

My father runs a shop, and the ERP software he was using was expensive, hard to understand, and didn't give him the reports he actually needed. That's what pushed me to build VenQore. Since then it's been running in two more small businesses. Separately, working as a virtual assistant managing inventory across eBay, TikTok, and Amazon for another company, I saw directly how overselling out-of-stock items across unsynced channels damages a seller's account standing. Small businesses are overpaying for complexity they don't need, missing reports they do need, and doing manual data entry that software should already be handling.

---

### Why this idea, why you?

I didn't start from a market report — I built this to fix my father's business, then kept extending it every time a real problem showed up. The recipe-costing module exists because we were manually tracking inventory for a business selling both raw ingredients and manufactured products from the same stock. VenSynQ exists because I built a WooCommerce store myself and needed it to talk to the ERP. AI Scan exists because customers were sending orders as handwritten notes and voice messages. I'm not a computer science graduate — my background is in linguistics — but I taught myself to build, and VenQore is my third solo-shipped product after Al Ujrat (a WordPress-based local services marketplace) and Protocol VII (a Node/React habit-tracking SaaS). Every feature in VenQore traces back to a specific failure I either hit myself or watched a real business hit.

### Competition

The real competition is Excel, WhatsApp-based order-taking, and cheap disconnected single-purpose apps — not other ERPs. Existing ERP software in our market is priced and built for businesses much larger than the ones we serve, so most small business owners either can't afford it or only use a fraction of what they're paying for. The honest alternative for our target customer isn't a competing product — it's continuing to do things manually.

---

### Why now?

Multimodal AI models only recently became reliable and affordable enough to turn a handwritten note or voice message into structured sales data — that's the technical foundation AI Scan depends on. At the same time, marketplace APIs have matured enough (we already have Amazon SP-API approval) that small businesses can realistically sell across 4-5 channels at once, which makes inventory-sync a mainstream problem rather than a niche one.

---

### Long-term vision

Right now we're focused on being the best possible ERP/POS core for small businesses running on too many disconnected tools. The larger goal is for VenQore to become the single system a business runs on — not by bolting on unrelated features, but by removing one real friction point at a time: the sale, then the channel, then the supplier relationship, then the storefront. Every module we've shipped came from watching one specific business hit one specific wall, and we intend to keep building that way.
