02 · Principles

The five laws

1 · No raw values in components

No hex, no px radius, no z-[…]. If the value you want isn't a token, the system is missing a token — add it to tokens.css first.

2 · Teal is the brand, not a highlight

Primary actions, active states and focus are teal. Nothing else is teal.

3 · Module colour is wayfinding, semantic colour is data

They never swap jobs and never share a surface.

4 · Nothing meaningful is colour alone

A negative figure gets a minus sign and parentheses. A status chip gets an icon and a word. Roughly 1 in 12 men can't reliably separate your red from your green.

5 · Marketing may be expressive. The product may not.

The fluid canvas, the laser flow, the serif italic — those live on public pages. Inside the app the only motion is state feedback. That restraint is the enterprise feel.

03 · Foundations

Colour

The teal was right in v1 and it is still right. It is a low-chroma colour — 0.071 in OKLCH, where a saturated brand blue runs 0.15–0.20. Low chroma reads considered rather than loud, which is exactly the register you want on a product whose argument is trust my arithmetic. It is also not the AI colour: every AI product in 2026 is violet-to-indigo.

04 · Foundations

Module accents

You asked how to colour-code features without the whole thing turning into a circus. This is the answer: eleven module hues at chroma 0.095 — below every semantic colour, so an alert always out-shouts the furniture. Every ramp is generated from the teal ramp's own lightness curve, which is why the contrast figures below land in a band five hundredths wide instead of wherever they happened to fall.

The rule that keeps this honest: a module accent never appears inside a data region. Not in a table cell, not in a chart, not in a status chip, not on a KPI figure. It lives in chrome — sidebar item, page-header rule, module badge, breadcrumb, empty-state art — and at most three surfaces per screen. A page tinted end to end in violet is not wayfinding, it is a theme.

The detail that matters most for this product

Set font-feature-settings:"tnum" globally. Tabular figures make every digit the same width so columns of money align down the page. In a financial product, proportional figures in a table are a typo you can see from across the room — and almost nobody remembers to switch them on. Add cv11 (disambiguates 1 / l / I) and ss01: small refinements, but they are what separates a site that used Inter from a site that specified it.

Do
  • Weights 400 / 500 / 600 only
  • Tracking as a function of size: ≥40px → −3%; 24–40px → −2.5%; 18–24px → −1.5%; body → 0; ≤13px uppercase → +14%
  • Instrument Serif italic, one word per page, marketing only
  • Mono for eyebrows, labels, data, counts — it makes numbers look measured
  • Measure 60–75 characters (max-width: 72ch)
Don't
  • font-bold. 700 is not in this system
  • Two serif words on one page — one is a signature, two is a style, three is noise
  • More than one weight jump per hierarchy step
  • Let the browser choose where a hero headline breaks
  • Leave a one-word last line

Nesting rule: inner radius = outer radius − inner padding. A 14px card with 16px padding takes a 6px inner element, not another 14px one. Concentric corners are the difference between designed and assembled, and they are free.

Space

4px base. Scale: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24 · 32 · 40 · 48 · 64 · 80 · 96 · 128. Grid 12 columns, 24px gutters, 32px page margin desktop / 20px mobile. Breakpoints 640 · 768 · 1024 · 1280. Design mobile-first at 375px.

Density differs by context, deliberately. Marketing is comfortable — 80px section gaps, generous card padding. The product is compact — 48px table rows, 40px controls, 24px card padding. A dashboard that breathes like a landing page wastes the screen the user is paying for.

08 · Foundations

Elevation

Four levels, no more. In light mode use shadow. In dark mode use surface lightness, not shadow — a black shadow on a near-black background is invisible, and reaching for one anyway is the most common dark-mode mistake there is.

LevelLightDarkUse
0 — flat1px border, no shadow1px rgba(255,255,255,.08)Table, inline panel
1 — card0 1px 2px rgb(10 11 15/.05)background → #12141ACard, tile
2 — raised0 4px 16px rgb(10 11 15/.07)background → #1A1D25Dropdown, popover, hovered card
3 — overlay0 16px 48px rgb(10 11 15/.13)#1A1D25 + 1px white @10%Modal, drawer, command palette

1px border or a shadow, never both. Shadows are always neutral-dark and never coloured — a teal-tinted shadow looks like a mistake at 100% zoom and a bug at 200%. SidebarItem.jsx currently ships shadow-xl shadow-indigo-500/20, which is both mistakes at once.

09 · Foundations

Z-index

The app carries 31 distinct hand-written z-index values, from z-[5] to z-[99999], and tailwind.config.js defines none. That is the root cause: there was no list, so every developer who hit a stacking problem picked a bigger number than the last one. These twelve names are the only legal values. Gaps of 100 leave room to slot something in without starting another war.

z-command1000Command palette, global search
z-toast900Notifications — must clear an open modal
z-tooltip800Always above the thing it describes
z-popover700Popover anchored inside a modal
z-modal600Dialog, confirm — scrim at 599
z-drawer500Right panel, filter drawer, cart — scrim at 499
z-dropdown400Select, combobox, autocomplete, context menu
z-rail300Left sidebar
z-nav200App top bar
z-sticky100Sticky table header, sticky section bar
z-raised10Hover-lifted card, sticky first column
z-base0Page content

Why this order. Roughly: how much of the screen a thing owns, then what has to be dismissable on top of what. A tooltip outranks a modal because you can have a tooltip on a modal. A toast outranks both, because a "Saved" confirmation that appears behind the dialog that triggered it is worse than useless.

Three rules that prevent the next thirty-one values

  • Every scrim is its owner's level minus one. Never a separate number.
  • If a thing is invisible, look for an overflow-hidden ancestor before touching z-index. Clipping happens during paint, before stacking is considered — no z-index can escape an overflow-hidden parent. This is the most misunderstood thing about z-index and it is why half of those 31 values exist.
  • Anything that must escape its container — tooltip, dropdown, popover — is portalled to <body>. Not raised. Portalled. You already ship @headlessui/react; use its Portal.

Motion — and the hover rule that fixes 184 files

Motion in this brand should read as mechanical precision, not playfulness. Things assemble, settle and lock. Nothing bounces, nothing wobbles, nothing overshoots. An accounting product that springs is telling you something untrue about itself.

The hover contract

The app has 184 hover:scale-* usages, seven of them scale-150. Replace all of them with this one table.

ElementHover response
ButtonBackground darkens one ramp step + translateY(-1px)
Card / tiletranslateY(-2px) + elevation 1 → 2
Table rowBackground → --vq-sunken. Nothing else
Sidebar / nav itemBackground + text colour. Never a transform
Icon (standalone)Colour only. Never scale
Icon (inside a button)Inherits the button. No independent hover
LinkColour + underline
Avatar / chipRing appears. No scale
Media thumbnailThe image scales inside a fixed-ratio overflow-hidden frame — the only legitimate scale-plus-clip in the system

Never scale anything whose parent has overflow-hidden unless the clipping is the effect. Everywhere else, overflow-hidden + scale is the sidebar bug. And never scale a button — it makes the layout feel unstable; at scale-150 it makes the layout feel broken.

Never animate

  • A number counting up on a financial figure. It reads as a slot machine. @number-flow/react is fine for a marketing stat, wrong for a ledger balance.
  • Anything that shifts layout after paint.
  • Ambient loops below the fold.

prefers-reduced-motion: reveals become instant opacity fades, the fluid canvas renders one static frame, ambient loops stop. One media query — it's already in tokens.css — and it is an accessibility requirement, not an option.

11 · Applied

Components

Buttons · height 44 · padding 22px · radius full · one primary per view
  • One primary per view. Two primaries is no primary. The current hero has "Select your business" and "Book a Call" competing at equal weight — pick one.
  • Height 44px minimum, 48px on mobile. That is the touch-target floor and it is not negotiable.
  • Padding 22px horizontal — buttons that hug their label look cheap.
  • Hover darkens one ramp step and lifts 1px. Never scale.
  • Focus: 2px offset ring in --vq-focus, always visible. Hiding it is the keyboard equivalent of hiding the cursor.
  • Destructive confirms name the object in the button label — "Delete invoice INV-2291", not "Confirm".
Input · height 48 · radius 10 · 16px font · label above, always

Login / auth

Single centred card, max-width 400, radius xl, elevation 2, on --vq-sunken. Logo 32px above it. No hero art, no fluid canvas, no gradient. The login page's job is to be fast and boring — it is the page users see most often and least want to look at. Every second spent making it beautiful is a second the user spends not working.

Empty states

13 · Applied

Charts

You ship three chart libraries: recharts 3.6, eleven @visx/* packages, and d3-array + d3-shape. Three ways to draw a bar means three sets of colour defaults, three tooltip behaviours and three axis styles — which is most of why reports look unrelated to each other. Keep recharts. Quarantine visx to the one component that genuinely needs custom scales or zoom, and never import d3 directly.

The palette is computed, not chosen

Eight categorical slots, assigned in fixed order and never cycled. A ninth series folds into "Other", becomes small multiples, or the chart is wrong. Slot 1 is the brand hue, so the first series in every chart is VenQore teal.

The bugs, by name

SidebarItem.jsx — the hover clipping you described

Three defects in one component, all on the same root cause.

// the row wrapper
className="… rounded-2xl transition-all duration-300 group relative overflow-hidden"
//                                                              ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

// the icon, inside that wrapper
<div className="relative group-hover:scale-125 transition-transform origin-center">

// a ring that extends 4px BEYOND the icon, also inside it
<div className="absolute -inset-1 rounded-full border-2 …" />

// a tooltip positioned OUTSIDE the wrapper entirely, also inside it

Grep checks for CI
